Lead Story

We publish fewer games, and we finish all of them.

By the desk at Tomasyy game · Filed 13 November 2026

Most studios measure output in titles per year. We measure it in titles we are still willing to put our name on three years later. That number is smaller, and we think it is the more honest one.

Tomasyy game is a small independent team building casual games for phones. Our method is unglamorous: prototype quickly, discard most of it, and only carry a game forward when the core loop survives contact with real players rather than our own enthusiasm.

Once a game ships, it stays in support. Compatibility patches, balance passes and content updates continue for as long as people are playing — which is a longer horizon than most release calendars assume.

The result is a short catalogue. We think that reads as confidence rather than shortage, and we would rather be judged on the games that exist than on the ones we announced.

The Studio

Three desks, one workshop
Discipline

Design written down first

Every mechanic is specified in plain language before an engineer touches it. If a rule cannot be stated in two sentences, it is not ready to be built.

Evidence

Measured, not assumed

Prototypes ship with instrumentation from the first build. Difficulty is tuned from completion and drop-off data, not from internal opinion.

Upkeep

Supported after launch

A released title keeps receiving compatibility work. A game from 2020 should still open cleanly on a phone bought last month.

“A game is not finished on the day it appears in a store.”

Featured Title No. 22 / 2026

Tomasyy — a puzzle with no clock.

Our flagship release is a hand-authored logic puzzle. There is no timer, no fail state, and no resource that drains while you think. Each board introduces one idea, then asks you to combine it with everything the game has taught you so far.

Hints are free and unlimited. Progress is written on every move, and the whole game runs offline — on a plane, in a lift, or on a train that lost signal an hour ago.

Doctrine

Four rules, not negotiable
I.

No manufactured waiting

Energy bars and cooldowns are not added to games that do not need them. Difficulty belongs in the puzzle, never in the shop.

II.

Price before the tap

Anything purchasable is priced and described before you commit. No invented countdowns, no recurring “limited” offers.

III.

Minimal collection

We gather only what is required to run and improve the game, documented plainly in our privacy policy.

IV.

Fix before feature

A known crash is repaired before new content starts. A broken build is not a roadmap item — it is a broken product.
